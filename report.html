Signal summaries

Covered-base means were 4.081578 for PNS SOX10, 0.775705 for CNS SOX10, and 3.609413 for PNS H3K27ac.

Do not compare these as normalized effect sizes. They are submitter-provided bedGraph values with track-specific coverage and scales.

The PNS and CNS differential files contained 21 and 0 window-overlapping records, respectively. Seven of eight processed genomic tracks had at least one record in the window. The FANTOM metadata independently recovered CNhs12073/donor1, CNhs12345/donor2, and CNhs12621/donor3.

Methodology

  1. Pin and verify. Download 12 GEO, FANTOM, and UCSC inputs over HTTPS and require the committed SHA-256 digest for every file.
  2. Freeze the locus. Derive the unioned rn5 RefGene span for Pmp22 (chr10:49,316,999–49,346,994, plus strand), then add 1 Mb flanks.
  3. Stream processed tracks. Count BED/bedGraph records overlapping the zero-based, half-open window. For signal tracks, calculate a base-pair-weighted mean over reported covered bases and retain the maximum.
  4. Reconcile donors. Select the three named Schwann CAGE libraries from the original FANTOM SDRF, preserving extract, sample, RIN, and file fields.
  5. Preserve provenance. Commit the full locus slice, QC table, donor extract, summary, and code; exclude large raw downloads while retaining URLs and checksums.

Interpretation

Descriptive evidence, not a causal test. The excess of PNS peak calls is consistent with peripheral-nerve regulatory occupancy near Pmp22. It is not a formal PNS-versus-CNS effect estimate: submitted peak sets can differ in threshold and width, bedGraphs are combined tracks, and the H3K27ac deposit contains one IP plus its input—not two biological replicates. No native-human perturbation, therapeutic selectivity, or RNA/protein effect is established.
